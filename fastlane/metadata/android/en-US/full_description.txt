Mirror your Mac to your TV, no extra box needed. Telebeam turns your Google TV or Android TV into a screen mirroring receiver for the Screen Mirroring already built into your Mac.

HOW IT WORKS: 3 CLICKS ON THE MAC
1. Open Telebeam on your TV.
2. On your Mac, click Control Center in the menu bar, then Screen Mirroring.
3. Pick your TV. Your Mac's screen appears on the big screen.

Nothing to install on the Mac, no account, no cables. Mac and TV just need to be on the same Wi-Fi. The TV shows these steps itself, so anyone at home can do it.

WHAT YOU CAN DO
• Mirror your MacBook or iMac screen to the TV, or use the TV as a big second display
• Present slides and spreadsheets in a meeting room or classroom
• Watch your own videos, photos and web pages on the big screen, with sound
• Sharp picture up to 4K, low delay, so the mouse feels right on the TV
• Your Mac's volume keys can control the TV volume
• Works with iPhone and iPad Screen Mirroring too

MADE FOR THE TV
Big, simple screens you control with the remote. Name your TV the way you like, so it's easy to find in the Mac's Screen Mirroring list. If something goes wrong, the TV tells you in plain words what to do: same Wi-Fi, choose the TV again, or set the Mac to mirror the whole screen.

WORKS ON
Made for Google TV and Android TV, such as TVs from Sony, TCL, Hisense and Philips, Chromecast with Google TV, Google TV Streamer, Nvidia Shield and Android TV boxes. Tested on a Philips Google TV; if it doesn't run well on yours, write to us during your free trial. Your Mac's Screen Mirroring is part of macOS and uses Apple's AirPlay technology; Telebeam is the receiver on the TV side.

PRIVATE
Everything stays on your home network. No ads, no tracking, no account, no data collected.

OPEN SOURCE
Telebeam is open source (GPL-3.0). Source code: https://github.com/sebstech24/mirror-tv . Based on AirPlay Receiver by jqssun and UxPlay; uses FFmpeg (LGPL).

GOOD TO KNOW
• Protected video (for example from streaming services that block screen recording) shows as a black screen. This is decided by the Mac and the streaming service, not by the TV.
• If your TV already has its own mirroring receiver, Telebeam picks a different name so both show up.
